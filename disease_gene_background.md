# FGFR3: Disease and Normal Gene Function

## Disease Background

*Achondroplasia* is an inherited skeletal disorder caused by specific activating variants in the *FGFR3* gene. These variants increase FGFR3 signaling, which interferes with normal bone growth, particularly at the growth plates. Achondroplasia is characterized by disproportionate short stature, shortened limbs, and other skeletal features.

## FGFR3 Gene and Normal Protein

The *FGFR3* gene encodes *fibroblast growth factor receptor 3*, a receptor tyrosine kinase involved in regulating cell growth, differentiation, and bone development. FGFR3 normally responds to fibroblast growth factors and helps regulate signaling pathways that control the development and growth of cartilage and bone.

## Normal FGFR3 Function

Normally, FGFR3 participates in signaling pathways that regulate the growth and development of cartilage and bone. Proper FGFR3 signaling helps control the rate of bone growth.

## Disease-Associated Mutation

The documented achondroplasia variant *c.1138G>A* changes the FGFR3 codon from *GGC to GAC*, resulting in an amino-acid substitution from *glycine (Gly)* to *arginine (Arg)* at position *380*.

This is a *missense mutation* and does not change the overall length of the FGFR3 protein.
